# Scientific method

Fourteen local NASA GeoTIFFs provide six regional elevation/slope patches and two long-term polar-average layers. `scripts/inspect_nasa_rasters.py` records source metadata; `scripts/build_site_metrics.py` applies raster scale and offset, then summarizes valid cells within 1 km. The processed file includes six separate terrain-study references and all nine NASA candidate-region centers, with coordinate basis, source paths, checksums, resolution and pixel counts.

Candidate coordinates are approximate representative centers from the Ohio University Lunar Planner and use positive-east selenographic longitude in the app. NASA's published region areas are rendered as area-equivalent circles for context; they are not true outlines. The polar map uses a lunar polar stereographic projection with a 1,737,400 m reference radius. The displayed terrain rasters are regional patches; unprocessed or missing fields remain unavailable.

All six local terrain study patches have compact 3D meshes generated from 5 m/pixel LOLA rasters. Candidate-region sunlight and Earth visibility are sampled from the global historical averages. Candidate elevation and slope are available only at Malapert Massif and Nobile Rim 1, where local rasters contain their approximate centers. Neighborhood medians describe nearby terrain, not a landing ellipse. Spatial minimum and maximum are not uncertainty bounds. The source rasters do not include terrain-uncertainty products. Independent GIS comparison of the derived values remains outstanding.

Historical sunlight and Earth visibility are averages across an 18.6-year modeled cycle. The separate simulation uses illustrative periodic curves and is not a date-specific prediction. No local SPICE kernels, terrain-horizon illumination model, power model or operational communications model are used.
