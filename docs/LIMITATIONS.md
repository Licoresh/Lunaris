# Limitations

- The sunlight and Earth-visibility maps show long-term averages, not conditions for selected dates.
- Date scenarios use illustrative periodic curves calibrated to NASA PGDA Product 69 long-term averages. Set times follow those curves; they are not actual ephemerides or forecasts.
- The solar-input index is a horizontal-collector geometry proxy, not irradiance, watts, or array output. Earth visibility is geometric and does not model antenna pointing, ground-station coverage, or link budgets.
- The simulation uses an assumed obstruction angle; it does not calculate local terrain horizons, terrain shadows, thermal conditions, or eclipses.
- The map contains separate raster patches, not continuous high-resolution coverage of the whole south pole. Zooming does not increase source resolution.
- The nine candidate points are approximate representative centers, not touchdown coordinates. Dashed circles show area-equivalent sizes, not official region boundaries.
- Sunlight and Earth visibility are 1 km neighborhood summaries at each candidate center. Elevation and slope are available at only two candidate centers; six separate local terrain patches remain explorable.
- The overview Moon is an artistic model used for geographic orientation. It is not a precision lunar reference surface; local terrain geometry comes from the LOLA products where coverage is available.
- No terrain-uncertainty rasters are included. Independent scientific validation of every derived measurement has not been completed.
