# Two-minute demonstration guide

1. Open LUNARIS at `/` and show the south-polar map. Pan, zoom and switch between historical sunlight, historical Earth visibility, elevation and slope.
2. Choose a terrain region such as Shackleton Rim or Malapert Massif. Explain that the NASA map shows regional raster coverage; focus markers are reference locations, not certified landing ellipses.
3. Open the 3D terrain view where available, then compare site summaries. Distinguish processed measurements from unavailable fields.
4. Run the lunar-window simulation and adjust its time and assumed-obstruction controls. State that it is an illustrative repeatable scenario informed by NASA historical averages, not an actual-date forecast or operational power/communications model.
5. Open `/missions` to show the CLPS catalog and, with network access, compare NASA/JPL Horizons Sun and Earth geometry at two approximate research points or intervals. Explain that the geometric preview does not model local terrain horizons or a radio link budget.
6. Show source links and PDF report controls. Mission outcomes and coordinates are included only where cited sources support them.

Do not describe the overview Moon mesh as NASA terrain. Historical averages and the illustrative simulation are not date-specific predictions.
